The Problem

You are the integration layer for your own life.

Your calendar knows your meetings. Your inbox knows your promises. Your group chat knows the carpool schedule. None of them talk to each other — so every day, a person has to manually reconcile six tools that were each built for one slice of who they are.

  • A mom running a household is also an employee.
  • A teacher grading papers at 9pm is also a parent deciding whether to make the school play.
  • A team lead closing a deal is also supposed to show up for date night.
  • Every existing tool forces people to manage these as separate lives. They're not separate — it's one life, one day.
The Solution

One list, reorganized as your day actually changes.

Daily-flow connects to the accounts that make up a real life — email, calendar, and beyond — and turns all of it into a single, continuously updated list of what matters today, organized around your own stated goals, not just what's on your calendar.

Not a dashboard

You don't interpret it. It's already interpreted — commitments and deadlines, extracted, not just displayed.

Not six apps

One list. A meeting moves, a message comes in — the list updates itself, not you.

Reads real commitments

An email, a calendar invite, a thread — anywhere a promise actually gets made, not just what someone typed onto a calendar.

Built around your goals

Two people with an identical schedule get different lists, because what they're optimizing their day for isn't identical.

How It Works

Connect → Extract → Prioritize → One List

  • Connect the accounts that make up your life — Gmail and Google Calendar today, read-only, one click, disconnect anytime.
  • Extract — every message and event is read for real commitments and deadlines using an LLM classifier, not keyword matching.
  • Prioritize — commitments are weighed against the goals you've told Daily-flow matter to you.
  • Surface — one continuously updated "Today" list, duplicate messages about the same commitment collapsed into one row, dismiss/complete built in.

Why Now

This wasn't buildable five years ago. It is now.

Two things had to mature together, and only recently did both arrive at once:

AI that reads unstructured text at scale

Reliably turning a messy email thread into "this is a commitment, due Thursday" wasn't possible at consumer cost and accuracy until now.

Mature APIs into where life lives

Gmail, Calendar, and beyond now expose the real-time, secure hooks (push notifications, delta sync) this needs — not just batch exports.

Market

Anyone whose day doesn't fit in one app. That's most people.

Beachhead first, then expand — not a broad blitz on day one.

  • Beachhead: working parents juggling a job and a household — tightest pain, highest willingness to try at $1/month, strongest word-of-mouth network (pickup lines, group chats).
  • Expansion 1: teachers — natural overlap with working parents ("lesson plan meets permission slip meets parent email").
  • Expansion 2: team leads/managers balancing a team and a life outside it.
  • Expansion 3: students juggling school, work, and everything else.

Why Us

The neutral layer no platform owner can build.

Big platforms are building AI into their own calendars, inboxes, and assistants — but each is building for its own walled garden. None of them can be the thing that sees a person's whole life across every platform they actually use, because doing that means sending their own users' attention outside their own ecosystem.

"That neutrality — genuinely spanning every tool someone uses, not just the ones one company owns — is the thing only a focused, independent product can do."
Trust, By Design

The thing that makes this whole category possible.

  • Least-privilege, read-only access to every connected account.
  • One-click disconnect that actually revokes the token at the source — not just a local flag flip.
  • A hard, engineered limit on data retention (35-day TTL on extracted content, 7 days and immediately scrubbed on dismiss) — never "kept forever by default."
  • Never sold, never used to advertise, never shared with the platforms the data came from.
